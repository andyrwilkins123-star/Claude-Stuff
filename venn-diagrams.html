What is a Venn Diagram?

A Venn diagram uses overlapping circles inside a rectangle to show relationships between sets. Each circle represents a set, and the rectangle represents the universal set (everything we are considering).

Circle A (blue) and circle B (red) overlap in the middle. The rectangle is the universal set, written as ξ (the Greek letter "xi").

Key Notation

SymbolNameMeaningExample
ξUniversal setEverything under considerationAll students in Year 8
∩IntersectionIn both sets (AND)A ∩ B = play football AND tennis
∪UnionIn either or both sets (OR)A ∪ B = play football OR tennis (or both)
A'ComplementEverything not in the setA' = do NOT play football
∅Empty setA set with nothing in itIf no one plays both, A ∩ B = ∅

Combining Notation

You can combine these symbols to describe more specific regions:

NotationMeaningRegion
A ∩ B'In A but NOT in BThe part of circle A that doesn't overlap
A' ∩ BIn B but NOT in AThe part of circle B that doesn't overlap
(A ∪ B)'Not in A and not in BOutside both circles
(A ∩ B)'Not in both A and BEverything except the overlap

Worked Example

Year 8 Sports Survey

ξ = {all 30 students in 8C}

A = {students who play football}   B = {students who play tennis}

A ∩ B = {students playing both} = 5 students

A ∪ B = {students playing at least one sport} = 12 + 5 + 8 = 25 students

A' = {students NOT playing football} = 8 + 5 = 13 students

(A ∪ B)' = {students playing neither} = 5 students (outside both circles)
